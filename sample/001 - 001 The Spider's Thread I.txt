The Spider's Thread — I

It happened one day. The Buddha was strolling alone, at his leisure, along the edge of the lotus pond in Paradise. The lotus flowers blooming in the pond were all as white as pearls, and from the golden stamens at their hearts an indescribably sweet fragrance spilled ceaselessly into the air. It must have been morning in Paradise.

Presently the Buddha paused at the edge of the pond and, through the gaps between the lotus leaves covering the water, happened to glance at what lay below. The bottom of Hell lies directly beneath the lotus pond of Paradise, so through the crystal-clear water the River of Three Crossings and the Mountain of Needles could be seen as distinctly as the pictures in a peep-box.

Then, at the bottom of that Hell, his eye fell on a man called Kandata, writhing among the other sinners. This Kandata was a great robber who had killed people, set fire to houses, and done every manner of evil, yet he could remember one single good deed. Once, passing through a deep forest, he saw a little spider crawling along the side of the path. Kandata at once raised his foot to stamp it to death, but then he suddenly thought better of it: "No, no. Small as it is, this too is surely a thing with a life. To take that life for no reason would be a pity, whatever else one might say." And so in the end he let the spider live.

As he looked upon the state of Hell, the Buddha recalled that this Kandata had once spared a spider, and he resolved that, in return for that one good deed, he would rescue the man from Hell if he could. Fortunately, when he looked beside him, a spider of Paradise was spinning a beautiful silver thread upon a lotus leaf the color of jade. The Buddha gently took the spider's thread in his hand and lowered it straight down, from between the pearl-white lotuses, to the bottom of Hell far below.
