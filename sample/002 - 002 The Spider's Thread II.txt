The Spider's Thread — II

Down here, in the Pool of Blood at the bottom of Hell, floating and sinking along with the other sinners, was Kandata. Wherever one looked it was pitch dark, and whenever something did glimmer faintly out of that darkness, it was only the glint of the needles on the dreadful Mountain of Needles, so the forlornness of it was beyond words. What was more, all around was as hushed as the inside of a grave, and the only sound to be heard was the faint sighing of the sinners. Anyone who had fallen this far, it seems, was so worn out by Hell's many torments that he no longer had the strength even to cry. And so even Kandata, great robber though he was, could only choke on the blood of the pool and flounder like a dying frog.

But then, one day, Kandata happened to raise his head and look up at the sky above the Pool of Blood, and what should he see but a silver spider's thread sliding down toward him from the far, far heavens through that silent darkness, a single fine strand glinting as though afraid of being seen. At the sight, Kandata clapped his hands for joy. If he clung to this thread and climbed and kept on climbing, he would surely escape from Hell. No — with luck, he might even get into Paradise. Then he would never again be driven up the Mountain of Needles, nor sunk in the Pool of Blood.

With that thought, Kandata promptly gripped the spider's thread firmly in both hands and began hauling himself up, higher and higher, with all his might. Being a great robber, he had long been well used to this sort of thing.

But between Hell and Paradise lie tens of thousands of leagues, and however hard he strained, it was no easy matter to reach the top. After climbing for some while, even Kandata at last grew tired and could not haul himself up a single handhold further. With nothing else for it, he decided to rest for a moment, and hanging from the middle of the thread, he looked far down below him.

His hard climb had been worth it: the Pool of Blood where he had been until a short while ago was now hidden somewhere in the depths of the darkness, and the dreadful, faintly glowing Mountain of Needles was beneath his feet as well. If he kept climbing at this rate, escaping from Hell might be easier than he had expected. Twining both hands around the spider's thread, Kandata laughed in a voice he had not used in all his years down here: "I've done it! I've done it!" But then he suddenly noticed that, on the lower part of the spider's thread, countless sinners were following in his wake, clambering eagerly up and up just as he had, like a line of ants. At this sight, from shock and fear, Kandata could only gape for a while like a fool, his mouth hanging wide open and only his eyes moving. How could this slender spider's thread, which looked ready to snap under him alone, bear the weight of so many? If it should break partway up, then even he — he who mattered most, who had climbed all this way — would plunge headlong back into Hell. That would be a disaster. Yet even as he thought this, the sinners came swarming up from the bottom of the pitch-dark Pool of Blood by the hundreds, by the thousands, climbing busily in single file up the faintly glinting spider's thread. Unless he did something now, the thread would surely snap in two in the middle, and he would fall.

So Kandata raised his voice and bellowed, "Hey, you sinners! This spider's thread is mine! Who said you could climb up here? Get down! Get down!"

That very instant, the spider's thread, which until then had held without the slightest trouble, suddenly snapped with a sharp twang right where Kandata was hanging. Kandata was done for. Before he could so much as cry out, he cut through the wind, spinning round and round like a top, and in the blink of an eye he fell headlong into the depths of the darkness.

Afterward there was only the spider's thread of Paradise, glinting faintly, hanging short in the middle of a sky without moon or stars.
