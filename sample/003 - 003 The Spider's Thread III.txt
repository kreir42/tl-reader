The Spider's Thread — III

The Buddha, standing at the edge of the lotus pond in Paradise, had watched all of this from beginning to end. Presently, when Kandata had sunk like a stone to the bottom of the Pool of Blood, he began to stroll on again with a sorrowful face. Kandata's heartless wish to escape from Hell by himself alone, and the fitting punishment that had dropped him back into the Hell he came from — to the Buddha's eyes, it must all have seemed pitiful and base.

But the lotuses in the lotus pond of Paradise cared nothing at all for such things. Their pearl-white flowers swayed their calyxes gently around the Buddha's feet, and from the golden stamens at their hearts an indescribably sweet fragrance spilled ceaselessly into the air. It must have been nearly noon in Paradise by now.
[TL Note: 萼 is normally read がく (calyx), but the ruby gives うてな, which also means the lotus pedestal (蓮の台) on which the Buddha and those reborn in Paradise sit. This paragraph echoes the story's opening almost word for word, with only the time of day moved from morning to near noon.]

(April 16, 1918)
